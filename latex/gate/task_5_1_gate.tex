\begin{center}
    \Large \textbf{Solution to Matrix Chain Multiplication Problem}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Question:}

Assume that multiplying a matrix $G_1$ of dimension $p \times q$ with another matrix $G_2$ of dimension $q \times r$ requires $pqr$ scalar multiplications. Computing the product of $n$ matrices $G_1G_2G_3 \dots G_n$ can be done by parenthesizing in different ways.

Define $G_iG_{i+1}$ as an explicitly computed pair for a given parenthesization if they are directly multiplied.

For example, in the matrix multiplication chain

\[
G_1G_2G_3G_4G_5G_6
\]

using parenthesization

\[
(G_1(G_2G_3))(G_4(G_5G_6))
\]

the explicitly computed pairs are:

\[
G_2G_3 \quad \text{and} \quad G_5G_6
\]

Consider the matrix multiplication chain

\[
F_1F_2F_3F_4F_5
\]

where matrices are of dimensions:

\[
F_1 : 2 \times 25
\]

\[
F_2 : 25 \times 3
\]

\[
F_3 : 3 \times 16
\]

\[
F_4 : 16 \times 1
\]

\[
F_5 : 1 \times 1000
\]

In the parenthesization that minimizes the total number of scalar multiplications, the explicitly computed pair(s) is/are:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
    \item $F_1F_2$ and $F_3F_4$ only
    \item $F_2F_3$ only
    \item $F_3F_4$ only
    \item $F_1F_2$ and $F_4F_5$ only
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Solution:}

The cost of multiplying a matrix of dimension

\[
(p \times q)
\]

with another matrix of dimension

\[
(q \times r)
\]

is:

\[
pqr
\]

scalar multiplications.

\vspace{0.3cm}

The matrix dimensions are:

\[
F_1 = 2 \times 25
\]

\[
F_2 = 25 \times 3
\]

\[
F_3 = 3 \times 16
\]

\[
F_4 = 16 \times 1
\]

\[
F_5 = 1 \times 1000
\]

Thus, the dimension array is:

\[
[2,25,3,16,1,1000]
\]

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Step 1: Compute Cost of Adjacent Matrix Multiplications}

\subsection*{1. Multiplying $F_1F_2$}

\[
(2 \times 25)(25 \times 3)
\]

Cost:

\[
2 \times 25 \times 3 = 150
\]

Resulting matrix dimension:

\[
2 \times 3
\]

\vspace{0.3cm}

\subsection*{2. Multiplying $F_2F_3$}

\[
(25 \times 3)(3 \times 16)
\]

Cost:

\[
25 \times 3 \times 16 = 1200
\]

Resulting matrix dimension:

\[
25 \times 16
\]

\vspace{0.3cm}

\subsection*{3. Multiplying $F_3F_4$}

\[
(3 \times 16)(16 \times 1)
\]

Cost:

\[
3 \times 16 \times 1 = 48
\]

Resulting matrix dimension:

\[
3 \times 1
\]

\vspace{0.3cm}

\subsection*{4. Multiplying $F_4F_5$}

\[
(16 \times 1)(1 \times 1000)
\]

Cost:

\[
16 \times 1 \times 1000 = 16000
\]

Resulting matrix dimension:

\[
16 \times 1000
\]

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Step 2: Choose the Minimum Cost Multiplication}

Among all adjacent pairs:

\[
F_1F_2 = 150
\]

\[
F_2F_3 = 1200
\]

\[
F_3F_4 = 48
\]

\[
F_4F_5 = 16000
\]

The minimum cost is:

\[
F_3F_4 = 48
\]

Hence, the optimal parenthesization should compute:

\[
(F_3F_4)
\]

first.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Step 3: Continue Remaining Multiplications}

After computing:

\[
(F_3F_4)
\]

the resulting matrix has dimension:

\[
3 \times 1
\]

Now the chain becomes:

\[
F_1(2 \times 25), \quad
F_2(25 \times 3), \quad
(F_3F_4)(3 \times 1), \quad
F_5(1 \times 1000)
\]

\vspace{0.3cm}

\subsection*{Multiply $F_2(F_3F_4)$}

\[
(25 \times 3)(3 \times 1)
\]

Cost:

\[
25 \times 3 \times 1 = 75
\]

Resulting matrix:

\[
25 \times 1
\]

\vspace{0.3cm}

\subsection*{Multiply $F_1$ with the above result}

\[
(2 \times 25)(25 \times 1)
\]

Cost:

\[
2 \times 25 \times 1 = 50
\]

Resulting matrix:

\[
2 \times 1
\]

\vspace{0.3cm}

\subsection*{Multiply with $F_5$}

\[
(2 \times 1)(1 \times 1000)
\]

Cost:

\[
2 \times 1 \times 1000 = 2000
\]

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Step 4: Compute Total Cost}

Total number of scalar multiplications:

\[
48 + 75 + 50 + 2000
\]

\[
= 2173
\]

This is the minimum possible cost.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Step 5: Determine Explicitly Computed Pairs}

An explicitly computed pair means two original adjacent matrices that are directly multiplied.

In the optimal parenthesization, only:

\[
F_3F_4
\]

is directly multiplied.

Therefore, the explicitly computed pair is:

\[
\boxed{F_3F_4 \text{ only}}
\]

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Final Answer:}

\[
\boxed{\text{(C) } F_3F_4 \text{ only}}
\]
